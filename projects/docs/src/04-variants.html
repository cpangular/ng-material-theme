Variants

Each palette colour comes with a family of variants. A variant is a step (lighter, darker, higher, lower) or a tint; reverse-mode and disabled are modifiers that combine with any of them. Every one except the tints has its own contrast colours, and the names follow one grammar.

Switch between Light and Dark at the top of the page. In the first row darker stays on the left. In the second, the end that stands out swaps sides. Reverse mode always shows the other mode's primary.

Lighter and darker

-lighter-1, -lighter-2 mix the colour toward white; -darker-1, -darker-2 toward black. They mean the same in both modes: darker is always darker. Each has its own contrast, named by adding --contrast: --color--primary-darker-1--contrast.

Higher and lower

-higher-1, -higher-2 stand out more against the page; -lower-1, -lower-2 less. The page is light in light mode and dark in dark mode (chapter 2), so higher is darker in light mode and lighter in dark mode. Use these for hover and pressed states, so a control that darkens on hover in light mode brightens in dark mode without a second rule. The button under that row uses -higher-1 on hover; try it in both modes.

Reverse mode

-reverse-mode gives the other mode's value: the dark primary where the element using it is in light mode, the light primary where it is in dark mode. color-scheme: dark from chapter 2 pins an element to dark; -reverse-mode instead always gives the opposite of the current mode, so it flips when the mode does. Use it for something that should always stand out against the page, such as a badge.

Tints

-tint-1 to -tint-9 are the colour at nine opacities, 2, 8, 12, 16, 25, 40, 55, 70 and 90%, painted over whatever is behind. Use -tint-2 as a hover overlay, -tint-3 for focus and pressed, and -tint-4 while dragging. Tints have no contrast colour; put text on the element behind them.

In the example the digits sit on the tints only as labels. On the stronger tints they read below 4.5:1, which is why real text belongs on the element behind.

Disabled

-disabled desaturates the colour and fades it, and -disabled--contrast fades the text with it. It is a modifier, so it combines with any variant: --color--primary-darker-1-disabled. A disabled swatch is faded on purpose and reads below 4.5:1 in both modes; it is meant to look unavailable, not to carry text that must be read.

Contrast with emphasis

Every colour and variant except the tints has three contrast colours. --contrast--high is pure black or white and reads at 4.5:1 or better on its colour, the WCAG AA level for normal text. --contrast keeps 90% of that black or white and mixes in 10% of the colour; it reads at 3:1 or better, the AA level for large text and interface parts. --contrast--low keeps 80% and is decorative: use it for hints and dividers, not for text that must be read. The low swatch in the example shows it on purpose.

The --contrast you used in chapter 1 is the middle one: fine for large text such as headings. For small text, use --contrast--high, as chapter 3 did.

The name grammar

--color--{palette}[-{variant}][-reverse-mode][-disabled][--contrast[--high|--low]]

{…} is a part you fill in, […] is optional, and | means one or the other. Single dashes join the parts of one colour; a double dash introduces its contrast. {variant} is one of lighter-1, lighter-2, darker-1, darker-2, higher-1, higher-2, lower-1, lower-2, or tint-1 to tint-9. neutral-variant is one palette name, so its tokens read --color--neutral-variant-lighter-1. Tints take no -disabled and no --contrast.

Read a full name left to right: --color--primary-higher-1-reverse-mode--contrast--high is primary, one step higher, in the other mode's value, and its strongest text colour.

Every percentage in this chapter (the mix amounts, the tint opacities and the contrast blends) is tunable; see Tuning. To look a token up, the Reference draws all of them.

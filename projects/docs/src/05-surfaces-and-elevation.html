Surfaces and elevation

Backgrounds for the page and the things on it, the text colours that are guaranteed to read on them, and six shadow depths.

The surface ladder

Surfaces are near-grey tones of the neutral palette from chapter 3, so they carry a trace of your primary's hue. --color--surface is the page. --color--surface-dim and --color--surface-bright sit either side of it. Five container tones stack on top:

Token Use
--color--surface-container-lowest Cards and sheets that should look brightest in light mode, nearest to black in dark.
--color--surface-container-low Grouped content behind cards.
--color--surface-container Toolbars, navigation.
--color--surface-container-high Chips, inline code, selected rows.
--color--surface-container-highest Input fields, the strongest fill.

In light mode the ladder goes from white down to light grey; in dark mode from near-black up. The relative order is the same in both, so a card on the page stays distinguishable when the mode flips.

Text on surfaces

--color--on-surface reads at 4.5:1 or better on every surface above, in both modes; that is checked for every hue. --color--surface-variant is a more tinted fill from the neutral-variant palette, with its own text colour --color--on-surface-variant, which also serves as the muted text on ordinary surfaces (captions, secondary lines). --color--background and --color--on-background are aliases of surface and on-surface, for stylesheets that prefer those names.

Outlines

--color--outline is a border that must be seen, such as a text field. --color--outline-variant is a quieter divider.

Elevation

--elevation-0 to --elevation-5 are box-shadow values of increasing depth. Zero is none. The shadow colour is --elevation-shadow-color, black by default; set it on :root to tint every shadow. Elevation is a shadow only: it does not change the surface colour, so combine it with a container tone as the cards do.

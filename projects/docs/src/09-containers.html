Containers

One mixin turns a palette, a variant and a style into a readable box. Everything it uses was introduced in chapters 4 and 5.

apply-container()

@include theme.apply-container($palette, $variant: null, $reverse-mode: false, $disabled: false, $style: filled, $border: null, $elevation: null);

It sets background and color, and when asked a border and a box-shadow. The palette and variant form a chapter 4 token name, so apply-container(primary, higher-1) paints --color--primary-higher-1 with its --contrast as text. $reverse-mode and $disabled add the matching suffixes; $border is a width; $elevation is 0 to 5 from chapter 5.

The three styles

$style Background Text Border
filled the colour its --contrast the colour
text transparent the colour the colour
normal inherit the colour the colour

Filled is a solid button or chip. Text is a text button, or an outlined one with $border. Normal is for an element that keeps its parent's background but speaks in the palette colour.

apply-container-vars()

The same arguments without the last two. It sets five variables and no properties, so the element and its children decide what to paint:

  • --container-bg-color and --container-color: the pair the style describes.
  • --container-border-color: the colour.
  • --container-color--high and --container-color--low: for filled, the colour's --contrast--high and --contrast--low; for text and normal, its -higher-1 and -lower-1.

The cards use it: a heading in the high-emphasis text, a caption in the low, both guaranteed to agree with the background because they come from the same palette slot.
